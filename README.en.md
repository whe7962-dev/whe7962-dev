### 🧭 About me

I'm **Winnie**, an **AI product manager** exploring the connections between product definition, experience design, and development.

I'm interested in how intelligent systems reshape the relationship between people and software: how intent is understood, how information becomes useful context, how decisions are explained, and how people retain judgment and control as tasks become automated. These questions shape the kind of trust an AI product can earn over time.

I see product design as a way to work with uncertainty: understand needs in context, turn assumptions into prototypes that people can examine, and revise decisions through feedback and observed results. I aim to connect product thinking, design, and development in a continuous process of testing ideas, with each iteration answering a clearer question.

This is where I share my work and thinking on how technology becomes an experience—and how efficiency, trust, and human needs can coexist within the same system.

> Define the ambiguous with clarity. Make the complex feel natural.

### 🛠️ Work in progress · Pet Partner

**A brand and web interaction prototype for science-informed pet care.**

The project explores how to organize pet care scenarios—from health questions and nutrition to everyday companionship—into clear product entry points.

- **Product communication**: organize five feature areas around concrete pet care scenarios.
- **Experience design**: bring together a companion character, everyday stories, and subtle motion.
- **Development practice**: build responsive pages with HTML / CSS / JavaScript, including keyboard support and reduced-motion preferences.

### 🔬 Ongoing interests

- **Context and product judgment**: selecting and organizing information with clear boundaries. How can a system get the context it needs at the right moment, while preserving uncertainty when evidence is insufficient?
- **Human–AI collaboration and trust**: explanations, confirmations, and ways to take control. Which decisions can be delegated to AI, and where should people be able to understand, choose, or intervene?
- **From prototypes to demonstrated value**: trade-offs between experience, quality, and cost. Test promising ideas in concrete tasks to see whether they reduce effort and improve outcomes.
